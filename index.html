    How it works

    Two formats, one rule: the picture is not your problem to fix, so it is not touched.

    HEIC / HEIF

    iPhone’s default camera format wraps HEVC-compressed pixels in an Apple container. ReWrap decodes the frames once at full resolution, draws them unchanged, and encodes JPEG once at your chosen quality. No resizing, no rotation, no filters, no second pass.

    decode → encode exactly one round trip

    ProRAW (DNG)

    Apple’s ProRAW files are DNG containers that embed a full-quality JPEG. ReWrap parses the TIFF structure, lifts that JPEG out, and re-wraps it — zero bytes of image data change.

    extract → wrap byte-for-byte

    Questions

    No resizing, cropping, rotating, color grading, or filtering is ever applied. For HEIC the pixel stream must be decoded and encoded once — JPEG cannot hold HEVC data — so that single round trip happens at quality 100 by default. For ProRAW the embedded JPEG is extracted verbatim: the bytes of the picture do not change at all. Metadata is copied as raw blocks, byte-for-byte.

    Two things. HEIC / HEIF — the default camera format on modern iPhones, and the file most people mean when they say “RAW”. And ProRAW (.dng) — the actual raw-format option, a DNG container holding sensor data plus a full-quality JPEG. ReWrap handles both.

    The Orientation tag is carried over untouched, and pixels are never rotated into it. Apps that honor EXIF orientation — Photos, Lightroom, most viewers — display the photo exactly as before.

    Usually, yes. HEVC compresses more efficiently than JPEG, so the same photo as a JPEG at quality 100 will typically be 1.5–3× larger. Metadata adds only a few hundred KB at most. That is the price of the format, not of this tool.

    ProRAW DNGs always embed a full-quality JPEG, so they extract cleanly. Some other DNGs store only raw sensor data — converting those requires demosaicing, which invents pixels. ReWrap refuses to do that rather than quietly change your photo.

    No. The decoder and metadata tools run locally in your browser. Drop in a hundred photos and disconnect the network — everything still works.
